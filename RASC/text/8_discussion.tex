\subsection{Discussion}
\label{rasc:sec:discussion}

\rev{\noindent\textbf{Action-length predictability and richer observability.}} Action lengths of some devices (e.g., shades) are more predictable than others, e.g., a thermostat  (HVAC) may have seasonal variations, oven cook time depends on food, etc. \Cref{rasc:sec:eval} evaluated {\it representative} devices that span this spectrum. 
More expressive observability (e.g., progress bars) may be feasible atop \abs{}, but it must be {\it simple} and {\it accurate}.

\rev{\noindent\textbf{Causality expressiveness.}} Current automation interfaces (e.g., Alexa~\cite{Alexa}, Google Home~\cite{GoogleHome}) support limited causality expressiveness.
Their IFTTT-style~\cite{ifttt} rules struggle with conditional, multi-stage dependencies.
Future work could integrate expressive, flow-based (and visual) systems like Node-RED~\cite{NodeRED} or  Flogo~\cite{flogo}.

\rev{\noindent\textbf{Privacy.} \abs{} does not alter the privacy posture of the deployment it runs in. All of its polling, lifecycle-state tracking, and scheduling operate within the same administrative domain as the underlying platform (e.g., a home running Home Assistant~\cite{HomeAssistant}): \abs{} introduces no new data flows that cross the domain boundary, sends no telemetry to third parties, and observes only device state that the platform already collects. A deployment that keeps its control logic on-premise therefore retains exactly the same privacy boundary with \abs{} as without it.} \revii{Domains with multiple users at differing authorization levels---e.g., a smart factory where contractors are only partially authorized while employees have full access---will already have privacy and access-control mechanisms of their own. These are orthogonal to \abs{}, but integrating with them (so that observability is granted only over the actions a given principal may see) is a prerequisite for deploying \abs{} in such settings.}

\revii{\noindent\textbf{Scaling to many users.} Smart spaces served by \abs{} range from homes to commercial buildings, and larger spaces have correspondingly more users. For \abs{}, additional users manifest primarily as \emph{scale}---more devices under management and more concurrent actions and routines---which is exactly the dimension \Cref{rasc:sec:eval} evaluates. Multiple users also raise concerns that are orthogonal to \abs{}'s abstraction, such as the privacy and authorization questions discussed above; addressing those is beyond this thesis's scope.}

\revii{\noindent\textbf{Maintaining conditions over long periods.} Some automation goals are \emph{standing} rather than one-shot---e.g., keeping the floor clean when a pet drops food repeatedly through the day. Such goals are typically programmed as a sensor trigger paired with a routine that is invoked on each firing, and are thus orthogonal to \abs{}'s contributions: \abs{} makes each invoked routine observable, whatever triggers it.}

\revii{\noindent\textbf{Exposing tradeoffs and costs to users.} The tolerance threshold $Q_w$ (\Cref{rasc:sec:rasc}) lets users trade detection latency against polling cost, but the value a user picks then applies uniformly: \abs{} offers no way to \emph{adapt} it to context. Different contexts warrant very different settings. Our evaluation used $Q_w=2$\,s for door actions, 3\,s for shades, and 30\,s for thermostat actions (\Cref{rasc:sec:detection_times}), and a household might reasonably want its front door watched at $Q_w=0.1$\,s while the house is empty but only at 10\,s while someone is home. Those two settings differ sharply in what they cost: at 0.1\,s \abs{} must issue many more polls and, under the minimum inter-arrival gaps that device APIs impose, may not be able to meet the threshold at all (\Cref{rasc:fig:rate-limiting}), whereas 10\,s is satisfied with far fewer polls and correspondingly less network and energy overhead. Supporting per-context thresholds---and showing what each setting costs before it is chosen---would let occupants tune \abs{} to their priorities as those priorities shift through the day.}
