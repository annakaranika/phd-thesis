\abstract{
The Internet of Things has instrumented billions of homes, buildings, farms, and factories
with smart devices, yet today's platforms fail to meet smart-space users' needs. Control logic sits in vendor clouds and hubs that add latency, cost, and
single points of failure; fire-and-forget remote procedure calls (RPCs) leave developers and
occupants blind on whether actions take effect; and users navigate a control interface
spectrum with no structured support. This thesis argues that smart-space control should be
designed around the needs of the people who occupy the space, across three planes of the
smart-space stack---the \emph{device action}, \emph{smart-space automation}, and
\emph{human interaction} planes.

We propose new abstractions and insights at each of these three planes, spanning distributed
systems and human-computer interaction. On the \emph{device action plane}, RASC
(Request-Ack-Start-Complete) replaces fire-and-forget RPC with an abstraction that exposes the
full action lifecycle atop existing platforms, so automations and occupants can observe and
program what their devices do; proofs and experiments confirm fast, low-overhead lifecycle
detection and large scheduling gains over state-of-the-art baselines. On the
\emph{smart-space automation plane}, CoMesh removes the hub bottlenecks, cloud dependency, and
outages of today's centralized control: it is the first fully decentralized, hub-less control
plane for large commercial edge IoT meshes, using group-based coordination with zero-message
exchange and locality-sensitive hashing to keep control local, scalable, and fault-tolerant.
On the \emph{human interaction plane}, we perform a study of real smart-home owners via
surveys and interviews, and derive insights showing that control is a fluid, situational
process shaped by reliability and cognitive load rather than ecosystem boundaries, motivating
how future smart-space operating systems should actually be built.
Though independent, the three contributions share one vision: each makes a plane of
smart-space control resilient, observable, and built around real user needs.
}
